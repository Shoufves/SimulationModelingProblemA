Definitive regime (from 2026) 
The CBAM definitive regime applies from 1 January 2026. Importers of CBAM goods or their indirect customs representatives are encouraged to follow the instructions available in the Authorisation Management Module section and submit their applications for the status of authorised CBAM declarant. 
EU importers (or their indirect customs representatives) who import more than the single mass-based threshold of 50 tonnes of CBAM goods into the EU are required to apply for the status of authorised CBAM declarants. Authorised CBAM declarants buy CBAM certificates from the national competent authority in their EU country of establishment. The price of the certificate is calculated based on the auction price of EU Emissions Trading System (ETS) allowances expressed in €/tonne of CO2 emitted, as a quarterly average in 2026 and as a weekly average from 2027 onwards.
EU importers declare the emissions embedded in their imports and surrender the corresponding number of certificates each year.
If importers can prove that a carbon price has already been paid during the production of the imported goods, the corresponding amount can be deducted.
Why CBAM?
Climate change is a global problem that needs global solutions. With the EU’s rising climate ambition, and as long as less stringent climate policies prevail in non-EU countries, there is a risk of ‘carbon leakage'. 
Carbon leakage occurs when companies based in the EU move carbon-intensive production to countries where there are less stringent climate policies in place, or when EU products get replaced by more carbon-intensive imports. 
CBAM ensures that a carbon price is paid for the embedded emissions generated during the production of certain goods imported into the EU. This ensures that: 
the carbon price of imports is equivalent to the carbon price of domestic production; 
the EU's climate objectives are not undermined. 
CBAM is designed to be compatible with WTO rules.

Support for developing countries 
The EU is committed to supporting developing countries and Least Developed Countries (LDCs) in greening their industries, transitioning to renewable energy sources and implementing CBAM. The EU is also committed to helping countries interested in introducing or enhancing their carbon pricing systems.

What is CBAM?            
CBAM complements the EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS). It helps prevent carbon leakage by ensuring that imported goods reflect the carbon emissions generated during their production.
In doing so, it supports the EU’s climate objectives, encourages cleaner industrial production in non-EU countries, and promotes global decarbonisation.What products are under CBAM?            
CBAM covers 
iron and steel 
cement 
aluminium 
fertilizers 
electricity 
hydrogen 
The information on each sector is on the CBAM sectors page.Where can I find the default values and benchmarks for 2026?
